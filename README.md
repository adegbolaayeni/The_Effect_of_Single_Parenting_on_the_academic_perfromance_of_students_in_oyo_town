# The Effect of Single Parenting on the Academic Performance of Students in Oyo Town, Oyo State, Nigeria

## Abstract

This study examined the effect of single parenting on the academic performance of secondary school students in Oyo Town, Oyo State, Nigeria. A descriptive survey research design was adopted, with a sample of 200 secondary school students drawn using stratified random sampling across selected schools in Oyo Town.

Findings revealed that while many students from single-parent homes demonstrate commitment and interest in their studies, they face significant resource and support deficits that constrain their academic outcomes relative to peers from two-parent households. The study concluded that single parenting does not automatically result in poor academic performance; rather, associated socioeconomic stressors — including financial constraints, limited parental availability, and inadequate institutional support — are the primary mediating factors.

---

## Research Objectives

1. Ascertain the academic performance levels of secondary school students from single-parent homes in Oyo Town.
2. Determine the academic performance levels of secondary school students from two-parent homes in Oyo Town.
3. Compare the academic performance of students across both family structures.
4. Identify perceived challenges faced by students from single-parent homes.
5. Suggest strategies and interventions to support the academic success of single-parent students.

---

## Methodology

| Component | Details |
|---|---|
| **Research Design** | Descriptive Survey |
| **Population** | Secondary school students in Oyo Town, Oyo State |
| **Sample Size** | 200 respondents |
| **Sampling Technique** | Stratified Random Sampling |
| **Instrument** | Structured Questionnaire (6 sections, 4-point Likert scale) |
| **Data Analysis** | Descriptive statistics, Independent-samples t-test, Cohen's d effect size |
| **Theoretical Framework** | Bronfenbrenner's Ecological Systems Theory; Coleman's Social Capital Theory; Family Stress Model |

---

## Key Findings

### Academic Performance Comparison

| Group | Mean Score (out of 4.00) | Percentage Equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| Single-parent students | 2.75 | 68.8% |
| Two-parent students | 3.05 | 76.3% |

- An independent-samples t-test confirmed a **statistically significant difference** between the two groups (*t* = −4.815, *p* < 0.001).
- The effect size (Cohen's *d* = 0.482) indicates a **small-to-medium practical difference**, suggesting family structure alone is not a deterministic factor in academic achievement.

### Notable Observations

- **70%** of students from single-parent homes demonstrated a strong interest in learning, indicating high intrinsic motivation.
- **57%** of single-parent students reported insufficient access to educational resources (textbooks, materials, internet).
- **50%** reported limited parental availability for academic guidance.
- **83%** of all respondents agreed that students from both family types have equal academic potential.
- Only **48%** of respondents found existing school support systems to be adequate for single-parent students.

---

## Theoretical Alignment

The findings are consistent with **Bronfenbrenner's Ecological Systems Theory**, affirming that academic outcomes are shaped by the complex interplay of microsystem (family), mesosystem (family-school interaction), exosystem (community resources), and macrosystem (socioeconomic policy) factors.

**Coleman's Social Capital Theory** further explains the observed performance gap through deficits in family social capital — reduced parent-child interaction time, lower intergenerational closure, and limited extrafamilial support networks — rather than family structure per se.
